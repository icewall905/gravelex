# Pebble Trainer reference

Use this reference for healthy adults, scaled to experience, goals, equipment, available time and actual recent workload. The reference describes population evidence. It does not establish an individual diagnosis or a required starting dose.

## Set the dose and the direction

WHO's adult activity target is 150–300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity per week, or 75–150 vigorous minutes or a mix, with major-muscle strengthening on at least two days. Any activity counts. Set smaller starting doses when the person's recent schedule calls for it. Gradually increase an element of training when current work is manageable and repeatable. [WHO](https://www.who.int/initiatives/behealthy/physical-activity)

ACSM's 2026 resistance-training stand finds that progressive resistance exercise improves strength, muscle size, power and physical function. For a beginner, use the equipment they will reliably use. Include the major movement patterns across the week, choose controllable repetitions and offer substitutions. For strength goals, heavier loading and multiple sets can help after a base is established. For hypertrophy, sufficient weekly volume matters. For power, choose loads the person can move quickly with good control. Training to failure, a precise set structure and a particular equipment type are optional for general progress. [ACSM position stand](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823/)

## Progress, recover and adjust

Use completed sessions, trends and the person's own feedback to choose the next workload. Track endurance duration and effort, lift sets and effort, completion and recovery alongside calendar constraints. Keep hard endurance and hard strength sessions from crowding each other when fatigue interferes with the stated priority. After interruptions, resume from the work the user can repeat and reassess before progressing. Missing measurements are unknown. Use a sustained pattern of recent load, sleep, symptoms and willingness to train. Avoid presenting universal weekly progression limits, acute-to-chronic ratios or individual wearable readings as injury predictions. [IOC load consensus](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27535989/)

## Produce a plan the person can follow

State why the plan matches their priorities and what trade-offs distinguish alternatives. For each session give warm-up, main work, effort or pace guidance and time. Use talk-test or simple perceived effort targets when reliable zones are unavailable. For lifting, give sets, rep ranges, rests and a progression rule appropriate for the user. Include a shorter or lower-intensity version when useful. Set a modest next checkpoint such as improved consistency, more comfortable pace at the same effort, or more repetitions with the same technique. Ask a focused question when a missing constraint would materially change the plan. Respect explicit restrictions and refer concerning symptoms or known medical restrictions to appropriate professional care.
